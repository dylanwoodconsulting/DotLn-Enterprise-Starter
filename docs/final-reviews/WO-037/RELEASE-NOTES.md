## Release overview

The soldier scene can now shoot. Select a few pieces, choose Aiming and shooting from the rail's new Fight group and click another glyph: streaks in each shooter's role colour cross the field, a spark marks every hit, the instruments count shots, hits and accuracy, and the target's row in the text layer counts the hits it has taken. Set the target running and the plain streaks fall behind it; give the shooters The firing solution and they meet it; turn up drag and they arrive late again; give them Iterative targeting and the hits return. A wall drawn across the lane ends every streak that reaches it with a chipped mark. A knockout setting retires a piece on the hit that reaches the count, and its squad closes up round the gap. A trial puts two aims on one seed and plots both accuracy series. The release is for anyone using the scene to see what it takes to hit a moving thing and what a wall does to a line of fire.

## Read before upgrading

Nothing needs migrating, and no dependency, Node or Angular version changes. Storage (`version: 1`) and the export (`scene-composition-v0`) keep their versions: a composition saved before this release opens and replays as it did, and a kept trial saved before it reads the four new metrics, shots, hits, accuracy and down units, as unknown. A composition saved by this release that uses a Fight tool is refused step by step by an older build, which does not know those kinds.

The two aims are arcade rules over the board's own recorded animation, not solvers. The firing solution and Moving targets read one recorded later pose of the marked piece and time the streak to arrive there; Iterative targeting reads later poses, up to its Later cues setting, as drag stretches the streak. In an aimed shot drag lengthens the streak's clip rather than slowing it, and the speed setting picks two, three or four beats. The inspector shows the pose frame and the clip each shot will play.

Three behaviours to know before you play. Aiming and shooting opens on its defaults after another tool was applied to the same pieces, showing Objective point, so choose the target again in Fire at before you press Apply. Hits to knock out counts every hit a piece has taken since the run began, so set late in a session it retires the target, and any bystander already past the count, on the next hit that reaches each; Restart replays the run from the start with the setting where it was applied. The Down units instrument counts the pieces in focus that are down, so it reads zero while only the shooters are selected; with nothing selected it counts the whole field.

Code that reads the scene engine's public types meets new members: `SceneFrame` carries `bullets` and `impacts`, a unit frame carries `hits`, `UnitActivity` gains `down`, `SceneMetrics` carries `shots`, `hits`, `accuracy` and `downUnits`, a step preview may carry a `shot` reading, and `TransformKind` gains `combat.fire`, `combat.aim` and `combat.projectile`. A painter or an exhaustive lookup over those needs the new cases. A frame with streaks in flight folds their positions and velocities into its hash after the units'; a frame with none hashes as before.

## Substantive changes

**Shooting.** Aiming and shooting gives the selected pieces a mark: another piece, chosen by clicking its glyph or in the inspector's Fire at list, or the objective point. Each piece fires along its aim at its rate while the mark is in range, with a shot speed, range, hit radius and knockout setting from the inspector. A streak is an entity of its own: it advances before the pieces move each frame, slows under drag when unaimed, and ends on the first standing piece other than its owner, at a wall or an obstacle, when its range is spent, or when an aimed streak's clip ends. A hit is counted on the shooter and on the piece struck.

**Aims.** Projectile trajectory sets a piece's shot speed and drag. The firing solution and Moving targets read one recorded pose of the marked piece and play the streak as a clip to it, so a streak meets a crossing piece; drag stretches the clip and the streak arrives late. Iterative targeting reads a later pose that matches the stretched clip, up to its Later cues setting. Without an aim tool a streak heads for where the mark stands when it leaves. The inspector says which pose a shot reads, how long its clip plays and whether its range cuts the clip short.

**Walls and marks.** A streak that meets a wall or an obstacle ends there with a chipped mark that fades over a quarter of a second. A wall drawn between shooters and their mark stops the volley, and the text layer counts the streaks in flight, the hit sparks and the wall and obstacle marks on the current frame.

**Knockout and squads.** Hits to knock out, off by default, retires a piece on the hit that reaches the count. A retired piece stops, stays on the field as a dim glyph, reads down with its hits in the text layer, takes no more hits and is no longer steered. A two-level squad that loses a member lays its pattern out again for those still standing, at its spacing and with its role costs, and a fixed squad whose leader goes down is led by the member that held the next slot. A piece taken out of a squad by another tool still leaves its slot empty, as before.

**Trials and instruments.** The strip gains shots, hits, accuracy and down units, and moves those four to the front once a shot has been fired. A trial compares accuracy as a series and draws the earlier branch's streaks dotted beside the live ones.

**The rail.** Five AI for Games rows gain typed transforms, Aiming and shooting, Projectile trajectory, The firing solution, Iterative targeting and Moving targets, and the rail's new Fight group holds them: thirty-three tools where there were twenty-eight.

## Progressive polish

Every new word on the scene and on the five rows' catalog pages is a message, including the board words their faces read in both places. The coach line steps aside once a Fight tool is applied. The inspector's tempo hint names the beat bands, a mixed selection reads Mixed speeds until the speed is edited, and the target chooser marks retired pieces unavailable. A path tool's inspector and Show search now follow the selected units' recorded routes through playback and pause after Restart, and preview Apply only before a route exists or after a path parameter is edited. Six scene visual baselines are regenerated for the wider instrument strip and the Fight group, and three are new. A few slow test sweeps were split into individual cases or given longer deadlines, with every assertion kept. The scene's architecture and rules documents describe the streaks, the aims, the knockout and the re-forming.

## Evidence and compatibility

Built on `v0.12.0` (`6eef542`). The registered review gate (install, lint with the workspace checks, type check, unit tests, format, build, browser and visual tests) passed on the released tree. The engine's suite passes 231 cases with 2 skipped, the scene's 198, the browser suite 186 and the visual suite 53, with axe at the serious and critical threshold and no horizontal overflow at 360, 768, 1440, 2080 by 1460 and 3840 by 1620. The flock's and the walls' known outcomes keep every frame hash and gain the four new series; a new known outcome pins a three-piece volley across clear ground and against a wall, and its hashes, streaks included, reproduce. The session this release is for was performed in the served production build in independent verification and again in the final review. The production build's initial bundle is 406.92 kB, and `package.json` and the lockfile are unchanged.

Known limits. The hit marker's look, a spark alone or a spark with a ring that fills toward knockout, awaits the operator's pick; the spark ships meanwhile. The three behaviours under Read before upgrading are recorded for a later change. Browser checks ran in Chromium only.
