# Add walls, obstacles and pathfinding to the soldier scene

The soldier scene's field had no geometry: its only walls were its edges, and the engine had no graph, no search and no path following. This change lets you draw walls and place obstacles on the field, give a squad A* or Dijkstra's algorithm from a new Ways group on the rail, and watch it route round what you drew, plan again when a wall lands across its way, and stop with a notice and a row that both say there is no route when the gap is sealed. A second squad can feel its way along a wall with Obstacle and wall avoidance instead of a route, two units sent exactly head on now part the same way every run, and a crowd under priority steering comes to rest instead of shimmering. Walls are steps of the composition, so they are saved, replayed at their frames and compared in a trial like any other step.

## What changed

The four commits read in order, each one layer; only the last commit's tree was gated, and the earlier three do not build on their own, because the engine's new metrics and kinds are read by the layers after them.

- **The engine.** A wall is a segment with a thickness and an obstacle a disc, held in `geometry.ts` as one shape model with three clearance queries (a body standing, a body moved in a straight line, a ray), so the tile graph's cells and edges, a search's two ends, smoothing, a follower's sight of its next waypoint, the stop at a shape and the avoidance rays all measure clearance alike and a route the search returns is one the body can walk. Geometry changes only through field steps (`field.wall`, `field.obstacle`, `field.clear`, `field.grid`) with their true tick and an empty selection, exempt from the no-units refusal as the objective is. `graph.ts` builds the eight-connected tile graph, or the same graph from a plain grid; `pathfinding.ts` runs A* with the zero, Manhattan, Euclidean and octile heuristics, Dijkstra's algorithm as the zero heuristic, keeps every expansion and smooths by line of sight; `path-following.ts` aims a unit along its route and plans again when a field step lands, the objective moves or the geometry stops the unit off its way. A unit with no route is blocked: nothing steers it, it brakes where it stands whatever else it carries, and it goes on as composed once a step gives it a way. `steering.ts` adds wall avoidance by a ray and two whiskers and a head-on policy beside collision avoidance, with `avoid` itself unchanged. Priority steering's separation group is repaired: what the neighbours add to a unit cancels between the two of a pair, and a unit's own steering does not answer for the speed the push gives it. The metrics gain path length, replans and blocked units.
- **The catalog rows.** Seven AI for Games rows gain typed transforms (A*, Dijkstra's algorithm, Path smoothing, Path following, Predictive path following, Obstacle and wall avoidance and Collision avoidance), the closed transform enum grows by `path.find`, `path.smooth` and `path.follow`, and the three lists of scene kinds name the same nine, which also brings the five rows already typed `path.cost` onto the rail: twenty-eight tools where there were sixteen. The catalog summary is rewritten to what the check computes. The Tile graph stays a background scene element with no faces, and its cell size is a field setting.
- **Older saved trials.** A kept trial saved before a metric existed restores and imports with that metric as a series of nulls, shown as unknown; the eight series the export first carried are still required and a later series that is present is still checked. Storage and the export keep their versions.
- **The page.** The transport gains the field tools (Select, Draw wall, Place obstacle, Clear walls and the Tile graph's cell size, whose name links its reading), and every shape is also a row of a list under the unit list, editable and removable from the keyboard. For a path tool the inspector reads out the routes, what the first search expanded and where it heads next, says in words what a cost function's weights do, and can shade the cells the last search closed and its frontier. Units with no route are named in a notice with the reason. The canvas draws walls as bars, obstacles as discs, the selected units' routes as faint dashed lines and the avoidance rays with their hits. Every new string is a message.

## Regenerated expected data

- `trial-known-outcome.json` was regenerated because the metric set grew. Every frame hash and every series it had is unchanged, compared field by field with the file at the base; it gains only the three new metrics' series, null path lengths and zero replans and blocked units, since a run with no field step moves exactly as it did.
- `walls-known-outcome.json` is new: the twelve units west of a wall given A* toward an objective beyond it, with a branch that seals the gap at frame 30. In the first branch every unit arrives with no frame inside a shape; in the second all twelve plan again and end blocked.
- The six scene visual baselines (`scene-360`, `scene-768`, `scene-1440` and the three `scene-flocking` images) were regenerated on macOS for the field tools in the transport, the eight instruments and the longer rail. No other baseline changed.

## Validation

- The registered review gate (install, lint with the workspace checks, e2e type check, unit tests, format, build, browser tests and visual tests) passed on the reviewed tree with no tracked-byte change, in the repair, and again in the final review after the four commits, whose last tree is that same tree. The engine's suite passes 217 cases with the two boarded cases skipped, the browser suite 181 and the visual suite 50.
- Both crowd cases fail at the base and pass here. The final review ran one probe against an export of the base engine and against this tree, on the spec's units and on the scene's own twenty: at the base no crowd of twelve under priority steering with Arrive comes to rest in 3,600 frames, and a settled crowd sent 2 to 12 units away fails to reach the objective inside 1,800 frames in 70 to 80 of 80 cases a crowd and is hundreds of frames late in the rest; here every crowd rests, and at each of 112 near objectives per crowd it reaches the objective no later than the same units under Arrive alone. The repair's own scan found none later on 261,630 near objectives across crowds of 2 to 20.
- A unit sealed off on the move comes to rest, stays, and moves again when the walls clear under each of eight compositions tried beside its path tool, Wander, Flee, Flocking, both avoidance tools and both priority orders among them.
- The session the change is for was performed three times in the served production build, twice in independent verification and once in the final review, at 360, 768, 1440, 2080 by 1460 and 3840 by 1620 with no horizontal overflow and no console or network error: draw a wall and two obstacles, route a squad round them to arrival, show the search, draw a second wall mid-run and watch eleven units plan again, seal the gap and read no route in the notice and the rows, compare the run with and without the seal, clear and arrive, save, reload and reopen with the walls, run wall avoidance and a head-on pair that parts with no collision and the same time on a replay.
- The production build's initial bundle is 406,919 bytes, inside the budget, and `package.json` and the lockfile are unchanged. The movement studies are untouched, so the Collision avoidance study and its spec read as they did.

## Review

The first independent verification failed the change on two engine defects, a settled crowd that reached a very near objective three frames after plain Arrive and a blocked unit carrying Wander that kept walking under a notice saying it had stopped; both were repaired and each repair has specs that fail on the earlier engine. The final review read the whole change, reproduced those findings as repaired, performed the session and made no source change. No draft pull request preceded this one, so no automated review or CI run on a draft was available to it; CI runs on this pull request.

## Limits and follow-ups

None of these blocks the change, and the first two are boarded in the specs as skipped failing cases.

- With Arrive retuned to a slow radius of 3 or less, a settled crowd under priority steering reaches a near objective one to five frames after the same units under Arrive alone. At the base such a crowd never reached it at all. The cause is not established.
- A two-level formation's anchor takes no route, so a formation sent beyond a wall walks at it and stalls there, and says so. A unit a formation step places leaves its route.
- A crowd under priority steering is first credited with arriving later than before the repair, by 2 to 31 percent across the five crowds the final review measured, because it now keeps its spacing and comes to rest where it used to churn.
- A squad given a path tool alone piles onto the objective, as plain Arrive always has; Separation or priority steering beside it keeps it apart.
- After Restart the inspector reads the routes as the ones Apply would plan until the tool is applied again, and the search is not shaded while that run plays.
- The danger, visibility, exposure and cover weights of the cost functions weigh layers that are uniform until an influence map lands one, and the inspector says so.
- The engine keeps its ray cast and wall avoidance out of its public API for now. Browser checks ran in Chromium only.
