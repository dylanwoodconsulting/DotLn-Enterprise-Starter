## Release overview

The soldier scene's field can now be built on. You draw walls and place obstacles, give a squad A* or Dijkstra's algorithm from the rail's new Ways group, and watch it find a route round what you drew, follow it, plan again when a wall lands across its way, and stop where it stands, saying there is no route, when you seal the gap. You can shade the cells the last search expanded and the frontier it left, give another squad Obstacle and wall avoidance and watch it feel along a wall with its whiskers instead of planning, send two units exactly head on and see them part the same way every time, and compare one seed with and without a wall. A crowd under priority steering now comes to rest. The release is for anyone using the scene to see how a route is found and followed on a field with geometry, where it fails, and what avoidance adds.

## Read before upgrading

Nothing needs migrating, and no dependency, Node or Angular version changes. Storage (`version: 1`) and the export (`scene-composition-v0`) keep their versions: a composition saved before this release opens as a field with nothing on it, and a kept trial saved before it reads the three new metrics as unknown.

Three things behave differently. A saved composition that uses Priority steering replays with different motion, because its separation group was repaired: the crowd keeps its spacing, comes to rest and is credited with arriving somewhat later than before, where it used to churn round the objective for ever. A composition that gives units Collision avoidance now parts a pair whose centres coincide or would meet exactly, where they used to pass through each other. A composition saved by this release that draws walls or uses a path tool is refused step by step by an older build, which does not know those kinds.

Code that reads the scene engine's public types meets new members: `SceneFrame` carries `geometry`, `routes` and `rays`, `SceneMetrics` carries `pathLength`, `replans` and `blockedUnits`, `UnitActivity` gains `blocked`, a step preview carries `routes`, and `TransformKind` gains `path.find`, `path.smooth` and `path.follow`. A painter or an exhaustive lookup over those needs the new cases.

## Substantive changes

**Walls and obstacles.** The transport's field tools draw a wall (press, drag, release; Shift keeps it straight across or down), place an obstacle (a click places one, a drag sets its radius) and clear the field. Every shape is also a row of a list under the unit list, with numbers you can edit and a Remove button, and a wall or an obstacle can be added there from the keyboard. A shape is a step of the composition with its true frame, so it is saved, replayed where and when it was drawn and compared in a trial like any other step. Walls and obstacles stop a unit dead in the direction that would meet them, as the field's edge does, and a unit a shape was drawn over can only move away from it.

**Routes.** The field's tile graph is eight-connected at a cell size you choose in the field tools (2.5 by default, so 48 by 32 cells). A cell is free when a unit can stand at its centre, an edge is open only when the unit moved along it clears every shape, and a corner step needs both of its side steps open, so a route the search returns is one the unit can walk. A* runs with the zero, Manhattan, Euclidean or octile heuristic and Dijkstra's algorithm is its zero heuristic; Path smoothing cuts a route to the waypoints in sight of each other; Path following and Predictive path following tune how a unit aims along it. A unit plans again when a wall, an obstacle or the cell size changes, when the objective moves, or when a shape stops it off its way, and the Replans instrument counts those.

**No route.** When no way exists the unit is blocked: nothing steers it, it brakes where it stands whatever else it carries, its row reads no route, the inspector's notice names it and the reason (sealed off, an objective too close to a shape to stand on, or hemmed in), and the Blocked units instrument counts it. It keeps everything it was given and goes on as composed once the field gives it a way.

**Reading a search.** For a path tool the inspector says how many of the selected units have a route and how long, how many cells the first one's search expanded and where it heads next. Show search shades the cells that search closed and the frontier it left. The cost functions' five rows multiply every step's cost by their travel weight, which changes how far the search trusts its heuristic and so how much it expands, never which ground is dear; their danger, visibility, exposure and cover weights weigh layers that are the same everywhere until an influence map lands one, and the inspector says which it is in words.

**Avoidance.** Obstacle and wall avoidance casts a ray ahead and a whisker either side, drawn on the field with a mark where each hits, and steers off the nearest surface without ever driving at what it feels. Collision avoidance is a rail tool of its own, with a head-on policy beside it: where the engine's avoidance has no direction to give, the pair takes one normal in world coordinates, the earlier unit in the scene's list one way and the later the other, the same on every run.

**A crowd that settles.** Under priority steering, what a unit's neighbours add to it is a stiffened push and friction that cancel between the two of a pair, and a unit's own steering no longer brakes the speed the crowd gives it. Twelve units with Arrive come to rest, and a settled crowd sent somewhere near starts at once and reaches it no later than the same units under Arrive alone, at Arrive's default tuning.

**The rail and the instruments.** Twelve tools join the rail's sixteen: A*, Dijkstra's algorithm, Path smoothing, Path following, Predictive path following and the five cost-function rows in a new Ways group, and Collision avoidance and Obstacle and wall avoidance in Avoid. The strip shows eight instruments, path length, replans and blocked units after the five it had.

## Progressive polish

The coach line over the field says how to draw while a field tool is chosen, so choosing one moves nothing on the page. The composition log tells drawing a wall, placing an obstacle, removing one shape and clearing the field apart. Each instrument lets its unit wrap beneath its figure in a narrow column. The Tile graph's cell size control links the Tile graph's reading in the catalog. The comparison leaves the route figures out of a trial in which no unit had a route. The scene's architecture and rules documents and the READMEs describe the field steps, the one shape model, the routes and the rules the scene keeps, and the six scene visual baselines are regenerated for the field tools, the eight instruments and the longer rail.

## Evidence and compatibility

Built on `v0.11.1` (`eb9d227`). The registered review gate (install, lint with the workspace checks, type check, unit tests, format, build, browser and visual tests) passed on the released tree: the engine's suite passes 217 cases, the browser suite 181 and the visual suite 50, with axe at the serious and critical threshold and no horizontal overflow at 360, 768, 1440, 2080 by 1460 and 3840 by 1620. The flock's known outcome keeps every frame hash, so a run with no wall and no path tool moves exactly as it did, and a new known outcome pins a squad's route round a wall and its blocked branch. The movement studies are untouched. The session this release is for was performed in the served production build in two independent verifications and the final review. The production build's initial bundle is 406,919 bytes.

Known limits. With Arrive retuned to a slow radius of 3 or less, a settled crowd under priority steering reaches a near objective one to five frames after the same units under Arrive alone; the cause is not established. A two-level formation's anchor takes no route, so a formation sent beyond a wall stalls at it. A squad given a path tool alone piles onto the objective as plain Arrive does; Separation or priority steering beside it keeps it apart. After Restart the inspector reads a path tool's routes as a preview until the tool is applied again. Browser checks ran in Chromium only.
