## Release overview

Two more works on the catalog's shelf can now be studied. Gödel, Escher, Bach and Team Topologies each have ten concepts with an authored study, which brings the catalog to a hundred and twenty. A study in either work is read as a small map: boxes for the things the idea relates and arrows for the relationships between them. Step through it and the sentence under the controls names the box or arrow in focus and says what happens there. Open Levels of description and step up from a refused edit to the plan that scheduled the work, each level answering its own question about one afternoon. Open Strange loop and step from a gallery to the print on its wall, into the town the print shows and back to the gallery you started in. Open Collaboration mode, X-as-a-Service mode and Facilitating mode and read how two teams exchange work and what ends each arrangement. The release is for anyone reading the catalog for the vocabulary of levels, loops and teams, and for a developer who wants to see a drawing family that takes every study as data.

## Read before upgrading

Nothing needs migrating, and no dependency, Node or Angular version changes. The reading history, the chosen location and every stored format are as they were. The new drawing family's types live inside the Tools feature, which exports only its routes, so no public type changes.

A branch that authors studies meets three stricter checks. A record, or a model a record registers, that reads a message key from another work's catalog now fails the workspace's study and message checks, because a fresh route loads only the selected work's catalog and the shared one. A relations drawing of a study whose model runs must name that same module as its executable, as a states drawing already had to. An RxJS reading in either new work must open with the illustrative mark, since the two works' mechanisms run as TypeScript.

One thing to know when reading. In the X-as-a-Service map two arrows meet and cross at Checkout's right edge beside a third arrowhead. The sentence under the controls names the arrow in focus at every step, so the map can be followed, but that edge is crowded.

## Substantive changes

**Gödel, Escher, Bach.** Ten studies: Levels of description, Chunking, Consistency, Gödel numbering, Decision procedure, Isomorphism, Sentence G, Jumping out of the system, Active symbols and Strange loop. Levels of description opens with one line of real test output and asks what it can and cannot tell you before it lays four records of one afternoon side by side. Strange loop opens with two cards to try, one that sends you to the room's windows and one that sends you back to its own letters, then traces Escher's Print Gallery with a link to the museum's image. Isomorphism joins two words by two routes that meet at the same code and sends you on to Gödel numbering, which runs the encoder.

**Team Topologies.** Ten studies: X-as-a-Service mode, Collaboration mode, Facilitating mode, Conway's law (1968), Reverse Conway manoeuvre, Homomorphic force, Team-scoped flow, Platform as a product, Organizational sensing and Seven-to-nine maximum effective team size. Each interaction mode follows one invented pair of teams to the point where the arrangement should end: an engineer who missed the joint sessions does the work from the published instructions alone, a second engineer runs the rollback drill without the coach, or a new case returns to self-service once the revised instructions cover it. Collaboration mode and Facilitating mode, which set a time box, also say what happens when it runs out first. Conway's law maps a graph of teams onto a graph of components, and its nearby link leads to the Reverse Conway manoeuvre, which starts from the design you want.

**The relations drawing.** The catalog's sixth drawing family draws a stack of levels read from the bottom up, with a returning arrow outside the stack for a loop, or a graph in two or three columns. One node or one arrow is in focus at each step, drawn heavier than the rest. Hovering an arrow names both of its ends. The named moments under the slider jump to a step and the current one is marked. At the last step, Back to the start returns to the first and keeps the keyboard on the slider. The map changes in place from step to step with no animation, and a reader who asked for reduced motion is offered no Play button.

**How a study in this family reads.** The worked example is shown open above the map instead of behind a disclosure, with a line of instructions for reading the arrows. Four studies open with a short sample to inspect first and a question whose answer the reader can reveal. The comparison with DotLn or with this app comes after the map under its own heading and says where it stops.

**Four mechanisms to change.** Chunking groups a five-event recording at a width you set from one to five and keeps the refusal count in view. Gödel numbering tests any candidate from 0 to 12 and draws the decoded word when the number is inside the encoder's image. Decision procedure connects or removes one wire of a lantern relay and answers whether two lanterns can be lit. Conway's law switches three design contacts and copies each into a dependency between the components their teams own. Each runs a small TypeScript model whose spec loads the study's own record. The other sixteen maps illustrate a relationship and say so.

**Study checks.** The study check and the message check hold each work's records and models to that work's own message catalog, hold a relations drawing to its executable module and require the illustrative mark in the two new works, as Read before upgrading describes. A corpus test pins the two works' twenty concepts and which four run.

## Progressive polish

The catalog's visual baseline at 1440 is regenerated for the two shelf counts, and the two narrower baselines are unchanged. The studies summary lists the two works. The catalog's architecture and rules documents describe the builder, the family, the two coverage rows and the rule for writing a relations study. The Tools test kit's copies of the message catalogs follow the app's.

## Evidence and compatibility

Built on `v0.13.0` (`08b4a7f`). The registered review gate (install, lint with the workspace checks, type check, unit tests, format, build, browser and visual tests) passed on the released tree. Run without the cache, the Tools suite passes 1,366 tests in 151 files, the tooling tests 98 and the browser suite 195, with axe at the serious and critical threshold and no horizontal overflow at 360, 768, 1440, 2080 by 1460 and 3840 by 1620. `npm run studies:check` reads 120 records with 0 problems. The session this release is for was performed in the served production build in independent verification and again in the final review. The production build's initial files total 406.92 kB, the new records, models and drawing load lazily, and `package.json` and the lockfile are unchanged.

The twenty studies are written in their authors' own words and quote no passage of either book. Two critics reviewed every study, one for facts and one for reading. The studies cite 55 public addresses, each opened on 2026-11-19 and again on 2026-11-21 and found reachable. A page may move after that. Every comparison with DotLn or with this app is an analogy the study states as one, with its limit beside it.

Known limits. The crowded edge in the X-as-a-Service map is recorded for a later change. The browser check of Organizational sensing reads the trial's duration in the step that approves the trial and not in the step that reports its results. The drawing's look was chosen through thirteen rounds of rendered pairs, two of which ended with neither candidate chosen. Browser checks ran in Chromium only.
